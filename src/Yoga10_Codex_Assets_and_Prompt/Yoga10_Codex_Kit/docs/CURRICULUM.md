# Ten-grade curriculum

This condenses the earlier 30-grade concept into ten learning themes with three illustrated focus poses each. It is a curated progression, and introduces advanced topics through supported preparation.

| Grade | Theme | Illustrated focus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Calm foundations | Easy seat, Mountain, bent-knee rest |
| 2 | Gentle spine | Tabletop, Cat, Cow |
| 3 | Standing support | Chair half-fold, supported heel raise, wall tree preparation |
| 4 | Standing poses | Warrior II, side angle, triangle with block |
| 5 | Floor strength | Bird dog, knees-down plank, bent-knee boat preparation |
| 6 | Gentle backbends | Sphinx, low cobra, low bridge |
| 7 | Hip mobility | Supported butterfly, reclined figure four, padded low lunge |
| 8 | Balance and reach | Shallow chair, supported Warrior III, supported dancer preparation |
| 9 | Teacher-guided preparation | Dolphin, grounded squat for crow, seated kneeling setup |
| 10 | Supervised integration | Wall shoulder press, half-split preparation, kneeling side plank |

## Ten hourly blocks

Hours 1-3 introduce the first pose through setup, brief exploration and controlled release. Hours 4-6 do the same for the second pose. Hours 7-9 introduce the third. Hour 10 closes with recovery and readiness reflection. For bilateral poses, use both sides comfortably within the same brief practice window.

Each hour contains twelve checkpoints at offsets 0, 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 35, 40, 45, 50 and 55 minutes. Their actions are view, read, prepare, try/observe, check, rest, refine, breathe, repeat/discuss, release, reflect and break.

For Grades 1-8, optional trial windows at minute 15 and minute 40 are capped at two minutes and one minute. These windows include setup, brief attempts, release and rest. Actual exertion may be substantially shorter. Hour 10 has no trial timer. Maximum available trial-window time is therefore 27 minutes per learning day, with no requirement to use it all.

Grades 9-10 have zero unsupervised trial time. Their corresponding checkpoints are observation and discussion with a teacher.

Progress depends on comfortable, controlled and repeatable practice. Finishing checkpoints records learning participation. Readiness can take many days or weeks, and advanced readiness needs a qualified teacher's assessment.
